Why the page looks like this

Five of six households surveyed named safety and trust as their main concern when hiring someone they do not know, and none named price. The page therefore leads with what has been checked and by whom, not with a price or a sales line.

Four of seven workers could not show a new client proof of past work. The record stays with the worker: the same link works whether the job comes through the platform or through a neighbour.

Built to open on a weak connection

Six of seven workers have mobile data only sometimes. The page is one file, under 50 kB, with no app to download, no login and no video. A household opens it from a WhatsApp message; a worker can send it themselves.

What a household cannot see

The worker's ID number, home address and phone number are held by the coordinator and never published. The page shows that a check happened, not the documents behind it.

Booking runs in WhatsApp because that is where both sides already are. A coordinator answers, so nothing has to be automated before the pilot proves people will book at all.

Deliberately not built yet

No app, no automated matching, no in-platform wallet, no worker-facing dashboard. A coordinator does the matching by hand and uses a payment link. If households do not book, none of that software would have been worth writing.

One spreadsheet and one checklist run the whole pilot. This is what the coordinator works from, and it is also where the pilot's evidence comes from.
